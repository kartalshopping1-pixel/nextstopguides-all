Kyoto has more than a thousand temples and shrines, so two days will never cover everything. The good news: with an early start and a sensible route, two days are enough to see the city's most famous sights, walk its prettiest streets and still enjoy a relaxed dinner. This plan splits Kyoto into an east day and a west day to avoid criss-crossing the city.

## Before you start: how Kyoto works

- **Go early.** The biggest sights are very crowded from mid-morning onwards. Being at your first stop by 7–8 am changes the experience completely.
- **Buses are slow and crowded.** Kyoto's city buses reach almost everywhere, but they get packed on popular routes. Use trains and the subway where you can, and walk short distances.
- **Temples close early.** Most close around 4:30–5 pm, so plan the evening for streets, markets and dinner.
- **Stay central.** Around Kyoto Station is practical for arrivals and day trips; the Shijo/Kawaramachi area is better for evenings.
- **Leave big bags behind.** Use coin lockers or luggage forwarding; many temple paths have steps.

## Day 1: Southern and eastern Kyoto

### Early morning: Fushimi Inari Taisha

Start at Fushimi Inari, the shrine famous for thousands of vermilion torii gates winding up Mount Inari. It is open around the clock and free to enter, which is why early mornings are the best time. JR Inari Station is only a couple of stops from Kyoto Station.

You do not need to climb to the summit. Walking up to the Yotsutsuji intersection, roughly halfway, gives you the best of the gates and a view over the city. Allow about two hours for a relaxed visit.

### Late morning: Kiyomizu-dera

Head to Kiyomizu-dera, the hillside temple with its famous wooden stage. The approach through the old lanes of Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka is part of the experience: traditional shops, tea houses and sweets. These streets get busy by late morning, so come back later or accept the crowds with good humour.

### Afternoon: Yasaka Shrine and Maruyama Park

Walk downhill through the lanes towards Yasaka Shrine and the neighboring Maruyama Park. This is a pleasant, mostly car-free walk, and there are plenty of places to stop for lunch along the way.

### Evening: Gion and Pontocho

Gion is Kyoto's best-known geisha district. Hanamikoji Street and the canal-side Shirakawa area are lovely at dusk.

> **Tip:** Be respectful in Gion. Some private lanes now prohibit photography, and residents have long asked visitors not to chase or photograph geiko and maiko. Follow the signs and keep your distance.

For dinner, cross to Pontocho, a narrow alley of restaurants running parallel to the Kamo River. Prices range from casual to very expensive, so check menus outside before you sit down.

## Day 2: Western and northern Kyoto

### Early morning: Arashiyama bamboo grove

Take the JR Sagano Line from Kyoto Station to Saga-Arashiyama and walk to the bamboo grove as early as you can. Like Fushimi Inari, it is free and open at all hours, and after 9 am it can be shoulder to shoulder.

From the grove you can easily visit:

- **Tenryu-ji**, a Zen temple with one of Kyoto's most beautiful gardens.
- **Okochi Sanso**, a former actor's villa with quiet gardens and views (entry includes tea).
- **Togetsukyo Bridge** and the riverside, where you can rent a boat in warmer months.

Arashiyama has many lunch options, from tofu restaurants to simple noodle shops.

### Afternoon: Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion

From Arashiyama, take a taxi or bus to Kinkaku-ji. The gold-leaf pavilion reflected in its pond is one of Japan's most photographed sights. The visit follows a one-way path and takes under an hour. If you still have energy, the rock garden of Ryoan-ji is a short distance away.

### Late afternoon: Nishiki Market

Return to the centre for Nishiki Market, a covered street of food stalls and shops often called "Kyoto's kitchen". Try pickles, grilled seafood, tamagoyaki and sweets. Many shops close around 5–6 pm, and the market asks visitors not to eat while walking — eat at the stall where you bought the food.

### Evening: free time downtown

Spend your last evening around Shijo and Kawaramachi, with its covered shopping arcades, izakaya and cafés.

## If you have an extra half day

- **Nara:** the deer park, Todai-ji's giant Buddha and Kasuga Taisha are easy to reach from Kyoto by train. It makes a full, rewarding day trip.
- **Philosopher's Path and Ginkaku-ji:** a peaceful canal-side walk in northern Higashiyama, especially beautiful in cherry blossom and autumn leaf season.
- **Tofuku-ji:** famous for its autumn colours and Zen gardens, close to Fushimi Inari.

## Practical tips

- **IC cards** work on Kyoto's trains, subway and city buses. See [Getting around Japan](post:getting-around-japan).
- **Comfortable shoes are essential.** You may easily walk 15,000+ steps; [blister bandages](packing:blister) weigh nothing and can save a day.
- **Seasons matter.** Kyoto is at its most beautiful — and most crowded — in cherry blossom season and in autumn. Summer is very hot and humid. See [the best time to visit Japan](post:best-time-to-visit-japan).
- **Tours and tickets:** if you would prefer a guided experience, such as a tea ceremony, a kimono rental or a guided walk, [Klook](partner:klook) lists many options you can book online.
