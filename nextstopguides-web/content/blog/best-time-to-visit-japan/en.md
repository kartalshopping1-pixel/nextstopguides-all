There is no bad time to visit Japan, but each season feels very different — in weather, crowds, prices and what you can see. Spring and autumn are the most popular for good reasons, while summer and winter have their own highlights and can be quieter in some places. This guide walks through the year so you can pick the timing that suits you, and it flags the national holidays that affect crowds and hotel prices.

A quick note on dates: nature does not follow a calendar. Cherry blossom and autumn leaf timings shift every year and vary by region, so treat the periods below as typical ranges and check the official forecasts closer to your trip.

## Spring (March to May)

**Weather:** mild, with cool mornings and evenings. Early spring can still feel chilly.

**Highlights:** cherry blossom (sakura) is Japan's most famous seasonal event. In Tokyo and Kyoto, peak bloom usually falls somewhere between late March and early April, moving north through April and reaching Hokkaido around late April or May. Parks fill with picnics (hanami), and temples, castles and rivers look spectacular.

**Keep in mind:**

- Cherry blossom season is extremely popular, and hotel prices in Kyoto and Tokyo rise sharply. Book accommodation early.
- **Golden Week**, a cluster of national holidays from late April to early May, is one of Japan's busiest domestic travel periods. Trains, attractions and hotels get crowded and expensive. Many visitors avoid it or plan around it.
- The blossoms themselves only last around a week or two in each place, and rain or wind can shorten that. Build flexibility into your plans.

## Early summer and the rainy season (June to mid-July)

**Weather:** warm and humid. Much of the main island, including Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka, has a rainy season (tsuyu), typically from early June to mid or late July. Okinawa's rainy season starts and ends earlier, while Hokkaido is largely unaffected.

**Highlights:** hydrangeas in gardens and temples, lush green landscapes and generally fewer international visitors than spring.

**Keep in mind:** it does not rain all day every day, but showers are frequent. Pack a [compact umbrella](packing:umbrella) and plan some indoor options such as museums, markets, aquariums and department stores.

## Summer (mid-July to August)

**Weather:** hot and very humid in most cities, with high temperatures that can make midday sightseeing tiring. The mountains and Hokkaido are cooler.

**Highlights:**

- **Festivals (matsuri)** happen all over the country. Kyoto's Gion Matsuri runs through July, with its famous float processions in mid and late July. Summer is also fireworks season.
- **Climbing Mount Fuji** is only possible during the official climbing season in summer; check the official information for exact dates, rules and reservations.
- **Hokkaido** is at its most pleasant, with lavender fields and hiking.

**Keep in mind:**

- **Obon**, a Buddhist holiday period in mid-August, is another busy domestic travel time.
- **Typhoon season** runs roughly from late summer into autumn, peaking around August and September. Typhoons can disrupt flights and trains for a day or two. Travel insurance and some flexibility help.
- Start early, rest at midday, and explore again in the evening. Stay hydrated and use sun protection.

## Autumn (September to November)

**Weather:** September can still be hot and typhoon-prone, but October and November are usually comfortable, dry and clear — many people's favourite time to travel.

**Highlights:** autumn leaves (koyo). The colours start in the north and in the mountains, reaching Tokyo and Kyoto typically from mid-November to early December. Kyoto's temples and gardens, Nikko, Hakone and the Japanese Alps are especially beautiful.

**Keep in mind:** peak foliage weekends in Kyoto are nearly as busy as cherry blossom season, so book early and visit famous spots at opening time. See our [Kyoto in 2 days](post:kyoto-in-2-days) plan for how to beat the crowds.

## Winter (December to February)

**Weather:** cold but often dry and sunny on the Pacific side, including Tokyo, with clear views of Mount Fuji on good days. Heavy snow falls in Hokkaido, the Japan Alps and along the Sea of Japan coast.

**Highlights:**

- **Winter illuminations** in city centres.
- **Skiing and snowboarding**, with world-famous powder snow in Hokkaido and Nagano.
- **Snow festivals**, such as the Sapporo Snow Festival in early February.
- **Hot springs (onsen)** feel especially good in cold weather — a night in a Hakone ryokan is a classic winter treat.
- Outside the holiday period, sightseeing is often quieter and hotel prices can be lower.

**Keep in mind:** around New Year (roughly 29 December to 3 January), many businesses, restaurants and some attractions close for several days, while shrines and temples become very busy. Trains are crowded at the start and end of this period.

## Quick summary

- **Best weather overall:** April–May (outside Golden Week) and October–November.
- **Most photogenic:** cherry blossom (late March–early April) and autumn leaves (mid-November–early December) — also the most crowded.
- **Fewest international crowds:** winter (outside New Year) and the rainy season.
- **For festivals:** summer.
- **For snow and onsen:** January and February.
- **Periods to plan around:** Golden Week, Obon and New Year.
